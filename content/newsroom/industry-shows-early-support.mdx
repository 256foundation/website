The 256 Foundation has three early backers, and they could hardly be more different.

Foundry, the Human Rights Foundation, and Heatbit each put money behind the same mission in the Foundation's first year: dismantle the proprietary mining empire, and fund the open source alternatives to a completely closed mining stack.

Foundry was first of the three, in August. The Human Rights Foundation followed in September. This week, Heatbit joined.

Three very different organizations, in the Foundation's first year.

## Why We Exist

Bitcoin's rules are open. The machines that secure it are not.

That is the gap the 256 Foundation was built to close. We formed in February 2024 and were recognized as a Tennessee 501(c)(3) that spring, with a mission set on day one: dismantle the proprietary mining empire, and fund open source developers to build open alternatives. Bitcoin's most physical layer, the machines themselves, runs on technology that a handful of manufacturers keep private.

The problems stack up fast. No datasheets for the chips. No open control board to start from. Firmware you cannot inspect for security or change to your liking. A pool layer you simply have to trust with share accounting. Closed source at every turn, for the industry that secures what is supposed to be a fully open and trustless monetary system. For as long as that holds, Bitcoin remains permissioned.

We knew what we were building toward from the first day. What we needed was the financial support to build it.

## An Incumbent Bets on Open

Foundry is one of the biggest names in Bitcoin mining. It runs a large-scale mining operation and one of the biggest mining pools in the world, and it is the kind of company that shapes how the rest of the industry operates.

Which is exactly why their support matters. When a heavyweight like Foundry puts money behind the open alternative to its own industry, that is not charity. It is a signal.

## A Human Rights Fund Reaches the Same Conclusion

The [Human Rights Foundation](https://hrf.org) funds Bitcoin where the tool protects people living under governments that treat money as a weapon.

In September, they named us in a Bitcoin Development Fund round, and made the case better than we could:

> The 256 Foundation's mission is to make Bitcoin mining free and open… Bitaxe provides an
> affordable entry point for home mining, offering protection against surveillance in
> authoritarian regimes, and enabling individuals to mine Bitcoin discreetly.

That is the whole argument. A miner someone can build with commoditized components, plug in, and run privately is harder to keep out of a dissident's hands than machines from a select few vendors that only work inside an industrial data center. Bitaxe, the open single-chip desktop miner, has already proven people want exactly that.

But the idea does not stop at a desktop miner. The point is bigger: every part of a mining system, at every scale, can have an open source alternative. Bitaxe opened the door. The Foundation exists to walk the rest of the industry through it, so that anyone can build a Bitcoin miner of any shape and size.

## The Proof in the Pudding

Then there is Heatbit.

[Heatbit](https://heatbit.com) builds Bitcoin miners that double as space heaters, a machine in a shape the closed industry never designed for. It has none of the incumbent advantages: no datasheet, no reference design to start from, no seat at the chip makers' table. It builds anyway.

Heatbit is the proof. It shows the pain is real, and that the gap is crossable, at a cost. Longer development, higher engineering and product bills, a ceiling on what the hardware can even become. A company with Heatbit's ambition should not have to spend its best years relearning what a manufacturer keeps to itself, in what is supposed to be an open ecosystem. Heatbit betting on open source is a bet that the next company like it doesn't have to.

## Three Backers, One Conclusion

A mining industry titan, a human rights fund, and an innovative hardware startup share almost no fine details, but support the same larger mission. They reached the same conclusion anyway: Bitcoin mining must be permissionless.

That is the point of doing this as a nonprofit. **Money from anyone, influence from no one.**

## What We Do With It

We take this support seriously. Their money is not going to sit. It funds real developers building real, open mining hardware and software. Work aimed at shaking up how this industry gets built. The kind of work that, done right, makes closed source the expensive choice.

They backed the mission. We intend to make them look right.

The first grant is already moving. We'll introduce it soon.
