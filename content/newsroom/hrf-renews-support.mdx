On August 31, 2026, the [Human Rights Foundation](https://hrf.org) awarded the 256 Foundation a new grant through its latest round of the [Bitcoin Development Fund](https://hrf.org/devfund), continuing HRF's ongoing support for our work building the open-source Bitcoin mining stack. This is not HRF's first grant to us. Lead Maintainers of Mujina Mining Firmware, Ember One Hashboard, Libre Board and Hydrapool Mining Pool are working hard to keep up momentum behind these community projects.

At Bitcoin 2026 in Las Vegas we joined the HRF stage for a panel on decentralizing Bitcoin mining and the open-source stack that makes it possible.

**Watch the panel →** [Decentralizing Bitcoin: The Solo Mining Movement](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=egF4J7L2iZ4)

We are grateful, and we want to be specific about the state of the work, because the honest story here is a good one.

## What's Already Working

Across our Core Projects, Ember One, Libre Board, Mujina and Hydrapool, real work is already in motion. [Mujina](https://mujina.org), the Linux kernel of Bitcoin mining, is being developed into [RY3T's Nova](https://nova.ry3t.com/en/), a home mining product now in development that runs surplus solar power straight to hashrate. (Disclosure: Schnitzel, Lead Maintainer of Libre Board, sits on RY3T's board.) Our hardware reference design, [Libre Board](https://libreboard.org), still needs remaining revisions and validation.

What's actually scarce is not interest. It is grant-funded maintainer time to review what the community is already building, ship it, and validate it properly. That is a more honest thing to fund than starting something new.

## From Reference Design to Hardware in Hand

Our hardware and firmware Core Projects are past the point of proving the idea works. What is left is finishing remaining revisions, reviewing community contributions, and getting a validated reference design in front of builders.

We are dismantling the proprietary mining empire.

## Why This Matters to Human Rights

Bitcoin is freedom money. It is secure, censorship resistant, and accessible to anyone with an internet connection, regardless of what government they live under or what permission they've been denied. Mining is not a side function of that promise. It is where the promise gets enforced.

Mining:

- settles every base-layer transaction
- issues every new bitcoin into existence through the block subsidy and fees

If mining hardware and firmware are closed, nobody outside a handful of manufacturers can verify that settlement and issuance are actually happening the way Bitcoin's rules say they should. An open mining stack means anyone can inspect it, confirm there are no backdoors, and trust the network's most foundational guarantees without having to trust a company.

Open source also means the stack is not built for one kind of miner. A closed, industrial-grade machine assumes access to power, capital, and infrastructure that much of the world does not have. An open mining stack lets someone build hardware that fits the energy and resources they actually have access to, and still compete for a share of network rewards. Combined with Hydrapool, our open mining pool, that means permissionless participation in the network for people who would otherwise be locked out of it entirely. That is why this work sits inside HRF's mission, not adjacent to it.
